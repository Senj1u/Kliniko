Kliniko Account and Data Deletion

Effective date: 13 October 2026 · Last updated: 15 October 2026

Use Settings → Account & Security → Delete account in Kliniko to clear local app data and delete the Firebase sign-in account. That in-app flow does not delete Firestore records or Firebase Storage objects left by earlier app versions. To request help with legacy cloud data or a failed deletion, email klinikoapp@gmail.com from your registered address.

Delete from the app

  1. Open Kliniko and sign in to the account you want to delete.
  2. Open Settings, expand Account & Security, and choose Delete account.
  3. Review the consequences and confirm the deletion.
  4. Reauthenticate with your account password when prompted.

After confirmation and recent-login verification, Kliniko cancels local notifications and its reminder task, clears the account's local database, prescription photos and account-specific files, removes account-specific preferences and its local encryption key, and then deletes the Firebase Authentication account. The app does not delete Firestore records or Firebase Storage objects. Request removal of cloud copies by email.

What is deleted

  • Your Kliniko Firebase Authentication account.
  • Local Isar records, including medicines, dose logs, appointments, prescriptions, health records and settings for this account.
  • This account's local prescription photos, account-specific preferences, encryption key and temporary files on this device.
  • Scheduled Kliniko notifications, legacy alarm timeouts and its periodic reminder task.

Current OCR and decrypted-image temporary files are account-scoped and removed during cleanup. Temporary OCR files from older app versions may remain if their account cannot be identified safely.

If local cleanup fails, the app identifies the failed step and does not delete the Firebase sign-in account. If local cleanup succeeds but Firebase Authentication deletion fails, the local data may already be gone while your sign-in account remains; reconnect and retry. The app does not claim that cloud records were deleted.

Cloud data and support requests

Current versions no longer read or write medicine or health records to Firebase. Cloud copies left by older versions are not imported or automatically deleted. If you administer the Firebase project, export anything you need first, then delete each account subcollection in the Firestore Console before deleting the parent user document. The app's former sync paths were users/{uid}/medicines, appointments, doseLogs, healthRecords, prescriptions, and settings. Check each subcollection for documents, then delete users/{uid}; deleting only the parent document does not delete its subcollections. Check Firebase Storage under users/{uid}/ for any older objects and remove them if present. Firebase Console manual deletion does not require adding a Cloud Function. Alternatively, email klinikoapp@gmail.com from the address registered to your account for help. Do not send your password or health details. Email is a manual support request, not an automatic deletion mechanism; wait for support to confirm any action.

Retention

Local Kliniko data is removed by the app when all local cleanup steps succeed. Signing out keeps the local database, photos, files and encryption key on this device, but cancels scheduled reminders. In-app account deletion does not remove legacy Firestore data or Firebase Storage objects. Data already disclosed to external providers, or retained in provider-managed backup systems, may be subject to their own retention schedules and cannot necessarily be recalled by Kliniko.

The app does not automatically open or assign records from a legacy unscoped local database because ownership cannot be verified. In-app account deletion therefore only removes the signed-in account's account-specific local database and files; the unscoped legacy database is not attributed to any account.
